The kit opens one scenario per lesson: the others are named word for word as the panel names them, so that all twelve can be reached from a lesson. Each lesson has four paragraphs of at most 95 words, a formula and three chips. A test keeps length from giving an answer away, in either direction, in each language: the right answer is the longest in 2 to 5 of the 12.

**Honest points.** Lesson 1 says the push is 6.6 mm† of water and that the flow starts well after the gain turns positive; it names the Low-tech Lab's panels as pumped, and home builds with no dimensions to copy. Lesson 2 gives the maker's +6 to +11 %† for a selective plate beside the model's +3 to +6.5 %†, and says why they differ. Lesson 3 says we found no measurement of the 70 %\* of heat that reaches its own layer. Lesson 4 says the reverse-flow threshold is a 1981 model of an upright tank, that we found no measurement of a flat collector below zero, that this model's figures rest on a formula for the sky, without which they fall to about a quarter, that the worst place is −0.61 m, and that the one-way valve buys no frost margin at the coldest point at y = 0. Lesson 5 says the model stops at the first crystal, follows no boiling, and that no peer-reviewed limit for glycol was found. Lesson 6 says the published years are a test plus a calculation, and that Davos's 65 % does not mean the machine survives there.
