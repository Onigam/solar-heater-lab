# The 3D of the Solar Heater Lab

State of the four worlds, written by `lab-modeler`, kit 0.6.0. What each is, how it is built, what is tested, what it costs, what remains. `docs/scenery.md` and `docs/water.md` hold the setting and the water.

**Not wired yet.** `src/lab.ts` belongs to the lead and still mounts the template's `device` world, and `src/data/parts.ts` still holds the template's three parts. The worlds run in a review page of their own: `npm run dev`, then `/src/three/review/index.html`, the kit's frame, the lab's simulation, part sheets that are stubs with the names of research §4. The block `src/lab.ts` needs is in §7.

## 1. The machine: one function, every world

`machine/unit.ts` builds the machine on its roof from `dims.ts`, in the roof's own frame (X across, Y out of the roof, -Z up the slope, `s` the distance up the slope). It uses `machine/collector.ts` twice and `machine/tank.ts` once. A world places its root and nothing else: `rig` on the bench, `house` on the tiles. `collector` and `tank-and-tap` call the same `buildCollector` and `buildTank`, and register their layers one by one. Tests compare them within 3 mm: the bank's triangles between `rig` and `house`, each layer of the collector, each part of the tank.

**Sections are geometry, not clipping planes.** `collector` opens on a stair at its top-left corner, each layer stopping short of the one under it; the section tool swaps in a true cut across the risers. `tank-and-tap` opens on the tank cut along its axis, since this is what the world is for; the section tool swaps in a cut square to the axis, the ten layers as slices of a circle. Nothing z-fights in layers millimetres apart, nothing needs capping, the kit's click-through of clipped faces has nothing to skip. The `rig` and the `house` show the closed machine.

**51 parts**, the ids of research §4, one list in `part-ids.ts`. `index.ts` exports `WORLD` (fields of each `SceneDef`), the four lazy modules and `PART_OPENS`.

### The test stand

At y = 0 the machine is the maker's, and only a graduated staff shows of the stand (its zero, in copper, on the collector's top), so that the part `test-stand` has something to outline when selected. Above zero the tank goes straight up, legs growing under it. **Below zero it goes beside the bank**, sideways first, then down, as research §0.4 asks, so it never passes through the collectors. It lands on two translucent battens, the same as the roof's, on four legs and two sills. A level line from the staff's zero runs under it.

**Where the drawing and the model part.** The model's pipes follow the slider in length: hot 0.35 + |y|, cold 2.3 + y (physics §7.4). The drawn pipes follow them above zero. Below zero the tank is beside the bank, so the drawn hot pipe also runs 2.2 m along the top of the bank, and the drawn cold pipe under the tank and back. The model has neither. The picture is honest about where the tank is, and the numbers are the model's: say it if a film holds on it.

### Flows

Dots at the state's true speed, `pipeSpeed`, signed, on `view.slowMo`, scaled by the section of each stretch: risers at about 1.4 cm/s when the pipes are at 12. Each coloured by the model's node where it is. **A floor:** while `flowDir` says the circuit flows, the dots in the pipes run no slower than 8 mm/s (`MIN_SPEED`), or a night's trickle would look still; when `flowDir` is 0 they stand. So the night's dots are faster than the truth, by up to a factor of six at the machine's own place: **a film that says « real time » must say it of the day.** Kit request 8 is this.

## 3. Attached and resting

Tested, each with its numbers in `machine/unit.test.ts`, `rig/scene.test.ts`, `house/scene.test.ts`, `tank/scene.test.ts`: the rail, the spacers and the tank's saddles on the roof's plane; the collectors on the rail's web; the case in its saddles; both pipes within 3 mm of their fittings at seven poses of the slider; neither pipe into the roof; the lines of the `house` and of `tank-and-tap` end to end; the machine's lowest vertex on the tile tops, within 2.5 mm, none below; the stand's legs from the paper to its battens; the bench's posts on the floor, the bench and the brackets gone when a part is isolated; the tank and all that is strapped to it moving with the slider; the clips that hold the cold pipe to the tray going with it.

Last reviewer's finds, and where each stands here: isolate leaving pipes standing: every pipe is a part, or in a part. Explode leaving things behind: same. Fog eating the machine: no workshop world has fog; the house has a far one, from 170 m. Posts holding nothing: no bench or bracket stays with nothing to hold, the left bracket goes with the section across. Trees like stacked boulders: `docs/scenery.md`. Gravel as polka dots: packed shapes of near tones, tested. Parts hidden when selected: `frame` and `viewDir` per part; the inner parts of the tank and the collector are seen through the world's own opening.

## 5. What the simulation gave, and what it did not

Everything the 3D reads is in the state; nothing was added to `src/sim`. `checkValve`, `pipeInsulation` and `site` are in `params`, which is why `house` and `rig` take a function that gives them. **`Math.abs(mdot)` in the thermal copy (`docs/sim-thermal.md`) does not touch the 3D**, which reads `pipeSpeed` and `flowDir`.

## 6. What still looks wrong, or is not done

- The bench is a device of this world, with no source: a section of the same roof on posts.
- Interior rooms of the house are bare but for the bathroom, the kitchen's sink and the meter box.
- The collector's blanket and the foam read by colour and light wear, with no fibre or cell texture.
- `air-gap` is a faint slab, an invention to give a name to what has no surface; its outline when selected is what shows it.
- Not walked at 768 px, nor in English. The kit's `walk.mjs` not run: it needs SwiftShader, the checks here were on Metal, at 1280 and 390 px, by scripts in the modeler's scratch space.
