# Physics of the Solar Heater lab

The simulation in `src/sim` (pure TypeScript, no DOM, no three.js, runs in Node under vitest). It models the class of machine of research §0.1: two flat collectors directly below a horizontal 300 L tank, a closed circuit of water and propylene glycol that climbs with no pump into a jacket round the drinking-water vessel. The maker's data sheet draws it; the maker is named only in the Sources panel.

The flow, the layers, the yield and the night's loss are **outcomes** of these equations, not curves drawn.

**Nothing is tuned to a reported figure.** The inputs were set before the comparison and not moved after it. Two inputs did change during the work, each for a reason unrelated to the comparison: the header's bore (K4 forces it, §1.3) and the number of nodes (a convergence check, §5). The pins of `src/sim/figures.test.ts` record what the model gives; a pin is not a target.

Words used for a number: **reported** (a source of `docs/research.md`, K or S number), **derived** (computed from reported figures, the computation is next to it), **estimate** (the sources are silent: a choice with its reason), **assumption** (a choice of how to run it), **model** (an output of this simulation). In the data shown to a visitor `†` marks a model result and `*` an estimate.

## 1. The model

All SI, temperatures in °C. Files: `machine.ts` (every constant of every machine, with its tag), `climate.ts` (sites, the day's sun, the air, the night), `plant.ts` (one instant and one step), `operate.ts` (the clock, the taps, the booster's timer), `runs.ts` (long runs), `presets.ts`, `types.ts`, `engine.ts` (`SolarSim implements LabSim`), `texts.ts`. The core is in `thermal/` (§2).

### 1.1 The sun and the air

- **Sun:** Cooper's declination, the hour angle, the incidence on a plane at any tilt and azimuth (Duffie and Beckman). Solar hours; no equation of time.
- **A clear day:** Meinel's beam `0.7^(AM^0.678)`, Kasten and Young's air mass, a diffuse of a tenth of the beam on the horizontal, isotropic on the plane, a ground of 0.2. Usual engineering values, an assumption.
- **A real day:** the month's irradiation (K50) on the day's clear-sky shape, at the clearness that gives the month's sum. Three modes of `params.sky`: the month's mean day, a clear day, and a **seeded series** of clear and cloudy days whose mean in each month is exactly 1 (`seeded` in `climate.ts`, a mulberry32: the kit's `rng` lives with three.js, which the physics must not import).
- **K50's two climates:** the monthly shape is PVGIS's, scaled to Keymark's annual plane (S11), the air shifted to its annual mean. An assumption, supported by K52, said once here. Other tilts: the ratio of two clear days at mid-month.
- **Air:** straight between mid-months, ±5.5 K, peak at 15:00, as LBL (assumption).
- **Beam against diffuse:** only the incidence modifier sees the split: the diffuse at about 60° (Brandemuehl and Beckman), K18 for the beam.

### 1.2 The collector

The curve K17 is used **once**, to find the local coefficients: `localCurve` solves for (F, u1, u2) such that a chain of 32 cells at the certificate's test flow returns the certificate at three points. Then the flow is free, and a slow fluid runs hotter and loses more (K39 comes from this).

```text
per m² gross of a riser node:   gain = F·G_eff − u1·(T − T_env) − u2·|T − T_env|·(T − T_env)
black (K17):                    F = 0.811,  u1 = 6.58 W/m²K,  u2 = 0.029 W/m²K²   (32 cells)
```

The capacity of a node is its fluid and a dry 6 kJ/m²K (a5 of other collectors, S11, S12, an estimate). A selective plate uses S10's curve on this machine's area: a simplification, the plate's colour is the only change.

### 1.3 The loop

A **chain of fluid nodes** round the circuit, in the order of the day's flow:

| Stretch      | Nodes | Height                                                                      | Volume                                                                        |
| ------------ | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cold pipe    | 12    | from the tank's bottom to the lower header                                  | 20 mm bore (estimate), 2.3 m at y = 0, follows y                              |
| Lower header | 1     | 0                                                                           | K4, below                                                                     |
| Risers       | 32    | 0 to L·sin(tilt), 0.97 m at 30°                                             | 70 tubes of 7 mm (estimate) over 1.943 m (reported)                           |
| Upper header | 1     | top                                                                         | K4, below                                                                     |
| Hot pipe     | 8     | top to the tank's bottom, up or down                                        | 0.35 m + \|y\|                                                                |
| Jacket       | 40    | up 0.22 m round the vessel, down again; both ports at the bottom (S27, S20) | what is left of K5's 19 L: 10.6 L (derived; K58 finds about 10 L another way) |

**K4 forces the header.** 3.8 L in a collector, less what 35 risers of 7 mm hold, leaves 1.25 L for two headers of 1.027 m: **27 mm** (derived), like LBL's 26.8 and the Cypriot 28. Research §3 wrote 18 mm; with 18 mm headers the risers would have to be 8.9 mm. Sensitivity in §5.

**Head.** `buoyantHead`: −g∮ρ dz, the sum over the nodes of ρ(T)·dz, signed by the flow's direction. A check: on S26's geometry it returns S26's closed form, eq. 12, to six digits (tested).

**Density and viscosity.** Water: Kell 1975, and a Vogel fit within 2.5 % of IAPWS (both tested at the printed points). Mix: the density table of S46 by mass and temperature, read straight. **The viscosity of the mix is typed from memory of the makers' and ASHRAE's tables**, no copy could be fetched, checked by eye against LBL's Fig. 2 at 60 %: _read the original before quoting it_. Specific heat: S46. Volume and mass percentages are not told apart (S46: within a point or two).

**Friction.** Laminar Poiseuille on 70 risers, the two manifolds (a third of the length as seen by the whole flow), the pipes; a slot for the jacket (12μL/wg³); Hooper's K1/Re + K∞ for 6 bends (K1 = 800, K∞ = 0.25, usual values). Past Re 2300 the Darcy factor blends smoothly into Petukhov's by Re 4000: **the peak in the pipes sits at Re ≈ 3,900 by day**, in the transition LBL also finds. The blend removes the jump that could drive a limit cycle; LBL ran both limits. Flow distribution among the risers: even, which is the first thing to doubt at low flow (§6).

**Flow.** `stepFlow`: I·dQ/dt = head − R(Q)·Q, implicit in R, stable and monotone at any step. **Reversal is not a mode: it is the sign.** A one-way valve is a zero for Q < 0, a K of 2 for Q > 0 and 5 Pa to open (estimate). **A real spring-loaded check valve typically needs far more than the whole push of 65 Pa, which would stop the loop for good; so this is a light swing valve, and that is a condition to state.**

**Fluid in the nodes.** `stepChain`: implicit upwind on a ring, O(n), no allocation, conservative to rounding at any step (tested). Fixed internal step, as S17 warns: 5 s in the live engine, 10 s in the runs of a day, 120 s in the runs of a year. Over a step longer than 10 s the flow may only settle by the share 10/dt: without this, a step of a minute finds a false strong flow at y < −0.7 m (`FLOW_SETTLE`, tested).

**Jacket to vessel.** 300 W/K (estimate, 150–300 W/m²K on 1.3 m²; S27 has the real correlation, not read), by the wall facing each layer.

### 1.4 The tank

Ten layers of equal height in a horizontal cylinder Ø0.44 m × 1.973 m (estimate, its length derived from 300 L; research rounds to 2.0): circular segments, 16 L at the ends, 37 L in the middle. Steel follows the water (about 33 kJ/K, estimate).

- **A jacket does not mix the tank.** A warm wall makes a warm film that rises to where the water is as warm as itself; a cold wall makes a cold film that sinks. `neutralLayer` delivers a share `stratify` of the heat there, the rest beside the wall. **70 % (estimate)**: no measurement of this tank. Unstable layers then mix (`stabilize`, `buoyancyMix` with a preset workspace).
- **Conduction** between layers through water and steel, **skin** to the air: the core's RC network, 2.4 W/K shared by area (derived from K14, §2.9 of the research).
- **Draw:** plug flow from the top, cold at the bottom; the inlet mixes the bottom layers holding 5.5 % of the volume (K40's draw-off mixing). Tempered: the tank gives less when it runs hot, to a load at 45 °C (K52). Over a whole year the demand is exact.
- **Booster:** a heat in the layer at half height (K6) under its thermostat, with the 83 °C cut-out (K9). `timer`: 16:00 to 19:00 (assumption).
- **Valves:** the combined valve at 99 °C, up to 10 kW (K10); the overheat valve shuts the loop at 85 °C (**estimate**, not published, 80–90 °C) and opens 5 K lower; the circuit's relief at 80 or 200 kPa gives a boiling point (Antoine + 2 K for the mix), past which the state says `vented`. **No boiling, no loss of fluid:** past that point the numbers are a flag.
- **Expansion:** the volume by Kell's density; what rises while nothing is drawn leaves by the cold relief (`reliefDrip`). The enthalpy of that water is neglected, a few percent of a small loss.
- **Direct circuit:** the chain has no jacket, and the water passes through the tank: out at the bottom, back at its own level above the return port (0.3 m, the Cypriot rigs). The head includes the tank's column. Reversed: from the port, back at the bottom. Energy exact.

### 1.5 The night

The certificate knows only the air. Below 50 W/m² a sink appears: the plate's loss goes to `T_env = T_a − δ`, where δ is the depression of a plate with no flow:

```text
T_sky = 0.0552·T_a^1.5 (Swinbank 1963, K)        h_sky = ε_g·4σT³
h_plate = 4σT³/(1/ε_p + 1/ε_g − 1) + 1 W/m²K      h_air = 2.8 + 3v (Watmuff)
back = 1 W/m²K                                    δ from the steady balance of plate, glass, air, sky
```

So the plate settles at the chain's temperature, and relaxes towards it at the certificate's rate (a little fast: the certificate's U holds for a plate hotter than its glass). ε_p 0.9 black, 0.1 selective, ε_g 0.88 (estimates). `clear` falls with the day's clearness (assumption) and with a passing cloud. **Dew or frost on the glass is not modelled, and would hold the plate up on a humid night**: this term is an upper bound, and `skyTerm: false` the lower one. The pipes see only the air.

### 1.6 The two clocks

`step(dt)`: the heat and the hour on `dt × timeScale`, in fixed steps of 5 s, at most 720 a frame. With `timeScale` at or below 1, the flow also relaxes on `dt`: at 0 the hour stops and the flow settles (tested). The same inputs give the same state (tested). A day per second costs about 1 ms a frame here. A step is about 4 µs on this machine, at 94 nodes.

## 2. The thermal core (`src/sim/thermal/`)

Copied from `sand-battery-lab` unchanged, with its tests. Its six shared files can move to the kit once the kit's `tubeFlow` is fixed (below, and `docs/kit-requests.md`); the four new ones wait for a release that holds them. On a throwaway copy running on the kit's 0.6.0 core, the 251 values the pins read come out the same to the last bit, and only two of the 203 tests of `src/sim` fail: the sign of `tubeFlow`, and the folder's rule of importing nothing from outside. One name clashes, `GRAVITY`, in the kit's `draft.ts` and in `loop.ts`, at the same 9.81.

**What changed in the copied files (2):**

| File          | Change                                              | Why                                                                                         |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `exchange.ts` | `tubeFlow`: `Math.abs(mdot)` in the Reynolds number | A negative flow gave Re = 1 (research §5.1, trap 4). One line, and the right physics: below |
| `index.ts`    | four `export *` lines for the new files             |                                                                                             |

**`Math.abs(mdot)` is the right physics, and no figure of this lab rests on it.** A straight tube has no front: reverse the flow and the same equations hold, mirrored. So Re, f and h read the size of the flow, the velocity alone keeps the sign, and `tubePressureDrop`, a size too, leaves the direction to the caller. The kit's 0.6.0 reads `mdot`, so any reversed flow gets Re = 1 and f = 64, whatever its speed:

| Reversed flow                                 | Closed form         | Kit 0.6.0              | This copy                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| −14.05 L/h of the mix at 20 °C, 20 mm, 1.69 m | Poiseuille: 3.73 Pa | Re 1, f 64: **424 Pa** | Re 114, f 0.56: 3.73 Pa           |
| −2 kg/s of air, 5 mm                          |                     | Re 1, f 64, h 19 W/m²K | Re 2.8e7, f 0.007, h 99,640 W/m²K |

The same flow forward gives the kit 3.73 Pa: its answer changes with the sign alone. **This lab's plant does not call `tubeFlow`:** its friction is `tubeResistance` at `Math.abs(p.Q)` (tested), and only `additions.test.ts` reads `tubeFlow`. One run on a throwaway copy of the plant, its tube friction taken from each law, shows what was at stake (clear June night at Athens, no test holds it):

| Tube friction from            | y = 0: peak, night, lost  | y = −0.61 m: peak, night, lost, plate − air |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `tubeResistance` (the lab)    | 132 L/h, −1.50 L/h, 4.8 % | 32 L/h, −14.05 L/h, 15.3 %, +1.7 K          |
| `tubeFlow` of this copy       | 122 L/h, −1.52 L/h, 4.9 % | 32 L/h, −14.04 L/h, 15.3 %, +1.7 K          |
| `tubeFlow` of the kit's 0.6.0 | 122 L/h, −1.15 L/h, 3.8 % | 34 L/h, −3.44 L/h, 5.8 %, −2.0 K            |

The kit's law would have hidden two thirds of the worst night's loss. **The plant keeps `tubeResistance` either way,** for two weaknesses of `tubeFlow` that have nothing to do with the sign: a jump at Re 2300, which costs the day's peak 8 % at y = 0 (`frictionFactor` blends it, §1.3), and a floor at Re = 1, which halves the friction of a slower flow (0.22 against Poiseuille's 0.44 Pa in the risers at −1.5 L/h, Re 0.5). **What neither copy holds:** the symmetry is that of a straight tube at one temperature. Buoyancy aiding or opposing the flow in a warm vertical tube is not modelled (§6).

**Not changed, and why:** `fluidLine` and `marchFluid` assume the fluid holds little heat, which is false at night; this lab does not call them, their comment says what they assume, and `loop.ts` says why it exists. `stableStep` ignores advection; this lab's fluid never goes through `stepNetwork`, and `stepChain` is stable at any step. `buoyancyMix` allocates three arrays; `layers.ts` has `stabilize`, the same pass with a preset workspace, tested equal to it.

**Two things the sweep shows that the research could not:**

## 6. Limits

- **One dimension.** The flow is a number round the loop. The risers share it evenly, and each node has one temperature. At a trickle of 2 L/h over 70 parallel risers, real collectors may circulate among their own tubes, which would change the loss of §4 in a way no 1-D model can say. A pipe's own convection (up one side, down the other, S17 has none either) is out.
- **Conduction along the loop** is out. At zero flow the hot pipe and the jacket do not exchange through the fluid or the steel.
- **The jacket's exchange** is a fixed UA, for any flow and either direction; S27's correlation should replace it.
- **The tank's stratification** is one number, not a flow in a tank. The cold water's jet is one number too. Enthalpy of the expansion is neglected.
- **The sky** is a formula from an unread paper, dew and frost absent, a mean clearness for a night; **a condensing plate would run warmer**: `skyTerm` off is the other bound.
- **No thermal mass of pipes' walls and no flow through the relief.** No boiling, no fluid lost, no glycol ageing at 116 °C. Above the circuit's boiling point the state flags it.
- **Frost stops at the first crystal.** No latent heat, no ice, no burst.
- **The weather** repeats day by day inside a month, clouds pass only if a scenario or a slider says so, no snow on the glass, no dirt.
- **A year's load** is a flat 200 L/day at 45 °C at three hours of the day; S20 varies it by season. Keymark's load is not printed.
- **`K21` is not a measurement** either. Nothing here is a measurement of the whole machine on a roof.

## 7. Assumptions that drive the results, for the owner

1. **The night sink on a clear sky (§1.5).** It sets the whole of the aha's size. Its removal divides the night's loss by four.
2. **The tank's layering (70 % to its own level, §1.4).** No measurement of this tank. A mixed tank doubles the loss below zero and takes 12 % off the year; `stratify` from 0.3 to 1 moves the year by −3 to +1.4 %.
3. **The jacket's height, half the vessel (0.22 m).** It decides the cliff below −0.6 m: a horizontal tank has so little height. A real mantle up to the top would move it a little.
4. **Pipes that follow the slider (hot 0.35 + |y|, cold 2.3 + y).** LBL kept its length fixed. It matters at the two ends only.
5. **The test stand is a test:** the real machine stays at y ≈ 0 (K30), where the model finds 4.8 % on a clear June night.
6. **A light swing valve (§1.3).** The valve's whole benefit depends on that.
7. **The collector's dry capacity and the load's shape** weigh little.

**State, flat, read every frame.** `flowLph` (signed), `flowDir`, `pipeSpeed` (true m/s, signed: the dots run at this, on `view.slowMo`), `headPa`, `headMm`, `loopT`, `loopZ`, `loopDz`, `loopMap` (`cold`, `lowerHeader`, `risers`, `upperHeader`, `hot`, `jacket`: start and count in `loopT`; 94 nodes closed, 54 direct), `collectorIn`, `collectorOut`, `plateT`, `plateMinusAir`, `coldestT`, `frostMargin`, `freezeT`, `layers` (10 °C, bottom up), `layerHeight`, `tankTop`, `tankBottom`, `tankMean`, `tankHeight` (y), `tankBase` (bottom of the tank above the lower header), `reliefDrip` (L/h), `tapFlow` (L/min), `tapT`, `boostOn`, `sunG`, `sunAlt`, `sunAz`, `airT`, `circuit`, `onDrawnMachine`, `alarms`. (`angle`, the template's placeholder, went when the lab was wired: nothing but the placeholder scene read it, and it fed no figure.)

**Dimensions.** `ROOF_UNIT`, `VESSEL_LENGTH`, `JACKET_HEIGHT`, `HEADER_BORE`, `JACKET_VOLUME` in `src/sim/machine.ts`: `src/three/dims.ts` imports them, as the sand lab's does. Moments as numbers: `flowStarted`, `reversalCount`, `showerCount`, `flowDir`. Two clocks as in §1.6. No random number that is not seeded, no wall clock. The cycle-phase item does not apply: nothing here has a cycle.

**Say to the writer.** The push is **about 7 mm of water** at noon, not 4 (K38). The flow starts near **07:10** on a clear June day, 2¼ h after the plate's net gain turns positive, because the plate must first climb back from below the air and past the cold column: not « when the gain beats the loss ». The worst tank in the model is at −0.61 m, not −1.22 m.
